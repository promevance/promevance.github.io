# Insights

The findings behind "Widening the Spotlight", with the numbers that support each one. All figures are from the Spotify track dataset supplied for the course unless stated. A **hit** is a track with popularity of 70 or more; **hit rate** is the share of tracks that are hits.

## 1. The catalogue is recent, and releases are getting shorter and more frequent

- 8,577 tracks, 2,545 artists and 5,200 albums, released 1952–2025. Average popularity 52; hit rate 27.7%; 25.0% of tracks explicit; average track length 3.5 minutes.
- **60% of tracks were released after 2015**, and 765 are from 2025.
- **Singles went from 7% of releases in 2010 to 58% in 2025.**
- The average track shortened from 3.9 to 3.1 minutes (about 50 seconds).

*So what:* artists now release in smaller, more frequent drops, so listeners lean on playlists and recommendations to discover music. Discovery, which Spotify controls, does much of the work albums and radio once did.

## 2. The spotlight is narrow

| Artist tier | Share of artists | Share of tracks | Share of hits | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Superstar (10M+ followers) | 8% (202 artists) | 42% | **58%** | 38% |
| Established (1M–10M) | 31% | 28% | 35% | 34% |
| Developing (100K–1M) | 27% | 14% | 7% | 14% |
| Emerging (<100K) | 34% | 16% | 0.5% | **1%** |

*So what:* success is concentrated. A handful of stars, and the labels behind them, hold strong bargaining power, and rival services stream the same stars.

Ten largest artists by followers. Bad Bunny has the best hit rate and is the only Latin act among them; BTS, the only K-pop act, has the lowest, a sign that K-pop's strength now lies in a broader set of artists.

## 4. Tastes are widening

| Release period | Hits | Latin and K-pop share of hits | Other superstars | All other artists |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010–15 | 487 | **0.6%** | 62.4% | 37.0% |
| 2016–20 | 624 | 3.2% | 65.9% | 30.9% |
| 2021–25 | 774 | **13.6%** | 45.5% | 41.0% |

- Latin and K-pop artists with a hit: **3 → 33** (2010–15 vs 2021–25).
- Superstar share of new hits: **69% → 56%** (2016–20 vs 2021–25).
- Industry context: Latin America was the fastest-growing recorded-music region in 2025 (+17.1%, IFPI); Latin music listeners on Spotify grew 986% since its Latin America launch (Spotify via MBW); global K-pop streams on Spotify grew 362% from 2018 to 2023 (Spotify via Korea Times); Bad Bunny was Spotify's most-streamed artist of 2025.

## 5. Latin and K-pop tracks are almost twice as likely to be hits

| Genre family | Hit rate |
|---|---|
| **Latin** | **54%** |
| **K-pop** | **46%** |
| Pop | 34% |
| Hip-Hop & Rap | 34% |
| Rock & Metal | 34% |
| R&B & Soul | 28% |
| Country & Folk | 24% |
| Electronic & Dance | 23% |
| Other | 23% |
| *All tracks* | *28%* |

Soundtrack & Classical (0%) is not shown on the dashboard chart. 39% of tracks have no genre in the source data and are left out of genre comparisons.

**Concentration risk.** Bad Bunny accounts for 55% of Latin hits; without him the Latin hit rate falls from 54% to 38%, still above the 28% average. K-pop is broader: without BTS its hit rate rises to 63%.

## 6. Why it matters to the business

Spotify's growth regions hold most of its users but fewer of its payers: Latin America and Rest of World are 58% of users but 39% of paying subscribers (Q4 2025, Spotify via MBW). Latin and K-pop are rooted in and heavily streamed across those regions, so serving them is one of the most direct ways to deepen engagement where growth is, and to give more listeners a reason to pay.
