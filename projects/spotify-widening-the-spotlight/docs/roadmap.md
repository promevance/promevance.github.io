# Roadmap: Widening the Spotlight, 2026–2035

> **Recommendation.** Spotify should serve the listener tastes growing fastest on its platform, Latin and K-pop, with the same discovery muscle it applies to its biggest stars, and grow a deeper pipeline of artists within them. This is not about moving the spotlight away from the superstars. It is about widening it, so that more of what listeners want gets heard.

Spotify does not make music and cannot change what people like, but it decides much of what they hear next. The plan therefore uses four levers it already owns:

| Lever | What it is |
|---|---|
| Recommendations | Autoplay, personalised mixes and the home screen decide what plays next |
| Editorial playlists | Curated playlists and home-screen shelves give a genre a front door |
| Artist programmes | Programmes such as RADAR (1,000+ emerging artists in 180+ markets since 2020) help new artists break through |
| Partnerships | Labels, agencies and fan events give listeners a reason to listen on Spotify |

## Horizon 1: Test and learn (Years 1–3, 2026–2028)

Evidence, not big bets. Pushing a genre to every listener would irritate as many people as it delights, so start where the fit is most natural and let the data decide how far to go.

- **Adjacent-taste tests:** recommend Latin and K-pop tracks to listeners of pop, hip-hop and R&B through A/B tests; expand only where saves and replays rise.
- **Deeper editorial playlists:** strengthen Latin and K-pop playlists and give both genres home-screen shelves in more markets.
- **Release-day features:** work with labels and agencies on countdown pages and launch features for major releases.

**KPI to watch:** Latin and K-pop share of new hits, **14% → 20%**.
**Evidence:** they rose from 0.6% to 14% of new hits, with hit rates of 54% and 46% against 28% overall.

## Horizon 2: Grow the pipeline (Years 3–5, 2029–2031)

Demand alone will not sustain the shift if it rests on a handful of names.

- **Dedicated RADAR cohorts** for Latin and K-pop emerging artists.
- **Audience data and tools** so artists and their teams see where listeners are and which songs travel.
- **Fan experiences:** sponsor events around tours and big releases.

**KPI to watch:** emerging-artist hit rate **1% → 5%**; no artist above 30% of a genre's hits.
**Evidence:** emerging artists have a 1% hit rate, and Bad Bunny alone accounts for 55% of Latin hits.

## Horizon 3: Repeat the playbook (Years 5–10, 2032–2035)

Latin and K-pop are today's rising tastes; others will follow.

- **Genre radar:** review the genre opportunity matrix every quarter for tastes with a high hit rate and a small presence.
- **Test and grow:** apply the same approach to each new candidate: adjacent-taste tests first, then artist development.
- **Keep the stars central:** superstars anchor the catalogue while the spotlight widens around them.

**KPI to watch:** overall hit rate **28% → 35%**; non-superstar share of hits above 40%.
**Evidence:** the superstar share of new hits fell from 69% (2016–20) to 56% (2021–25); tastes keep widening.

## Implementation plan

| Initiative | Suggested lead team | Timeline | KPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjacent-taste recommendation tests | Personalisation & Recommendations | 2026–2027 | Saves and replays in test groups |
| Latin and K-pop playlists and home-screen shelves | Editorial | 2026–2028 | Latin and K-pop share of new hits |
| Release-day features with labels and agencies | Artist & Label Partnerships | 2027–2028 | Hits per major release |
| Emerging-artist programme for Latin and K-pop | Artist Partnerships | 2029–2031 | Emerging-artist hit rate |
| Audience data and marketing tools for artists | Spotify for Artists | 2029–2031 | Artists with a hit per genre |
| Fan experiences around tours and releases | Marketing & Partnerships | 2029–2031 | Engagement around events |
| Quarterly genre radar | Data & Insights | 2032–2035 | Overall hit rate; non-superstar share of hits |

*Lead teams are suggestions based on how streaming platforms typically organise these functions.*

## Expected results

| KPI | Today | Target | By |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latin and K-pop share of new hits | 14% | 20% | 2028 |
| Emerging-artist hit rate | 1% | 5% | 2031 |
| Largest artist's share of a genre's hits | 55% (Latin) | below 30% | 2031 |
| Overall hit rate | 28% | 35% | 2035 |
| Non-superstar share of new hits | 44% | above 40% | ongoing |
| Paying share of users (Spotify-reported) | 39% | above 40% | 2031 |

Targets are proposals for discussion, based on 2010–2025 trends in the dataset and Spotify's 2025 results.

Potential benefits: stronger retention, less concentration risk, a sharper edge over rivals through better discovery, first access to new stars, and a stronger presence in rising music cultures.

## Risks and how to manage them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Listener fatigue**: pushing a genre too hard could annoy listeners | Start with nearby tastes, test before scaling, stop where engagement falls |
| **Dependence on one star**: Latin leans on Bad Bunny | The pipeline horizon targets no artist above 30% of a genre's hits |
| **Cultural authenticity**: genres are tied to communities | Local editorial teams and partnerships with the labels and agencies that know them |
| **Measuring the wrong thing**: popularity is a proxy | Confirm results with Spotify's own data on saves, replays, retention and subscriptions |
